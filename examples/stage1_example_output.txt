=== HyDE doc ===
Each electric motor and its dedicated battery pack and controller must be designed such that no single failure in any one propulsion unit or its associated power source results in loss of thrust from more than that unit. The shared coolant loop shall, following any single failure (e.g., pump, valve, or leak), maintain adequate cooling to all remaining motors to permit continued safe flight and landing. The flight control computer system, despite triple redundancy, shall not depend on a single power bus; an alternate or backup power supply path independent of the primary 28V DC bus must be provided to at least two of the three computers. Additionally, the absence of a common power bus between motor groups does not by itself satisfy propulsion system segregation requirements unless the grouping ensures that no credible electrical fault can disable more than one motor per group.

## Suggested Fixes

### 1. Flight‑Control Computer Power Supply
- Provide at least two independent power sources (e.g., an additional dedicated bus fed from a separate battery or from a different generator source) such that at least two of the three flight‑control computers are powered by a bus independent of the primary 28 V DC bus.
- Alternatively, equip each computer with its own dedicated backup battery or secondary power input, with automatic switching upon failure of the primary bus.
- Ensure the separation meets common‑cause failure requirements (lightning, HIRF, physical damage).

### 2. Shared Coolant Loop
- Redesign the cooling system to provide at least two independent coolant loops, each covering a subset of motors (e.g., one loop per side or per group), so that a single failure compromises cooling for only half the power units.
- Alternatively, prove by analysis and test that a single failure in the shared loop (e.g., a pump failure or hose rupture) does not lead to loss of more than one motor’s thrust capability (e.g., through passive convection cooling, thermal inertia, or a limited-time ****operating mode). This is unlikely with a single loop serving all eight motors, so the split‑loop approach is recommended.
- Ensure that each independent loop has its own pump, expansion tank, and monitoring.

## Regulatory Gaps Found

The provided documents do not contain explicit quantitative requirements for **cooling‑system redundancy** or **flight‑control power bus architecture** in the context of distributed electrical propulsion for VTOL aircraft. The requirements cited are derived from general **independence** and **single‑failure‑criterion** clauses (VTOL.2430(a)(1), VTOL.2510(a)(1)). While the design is clearly non‑compliant under those clauses, no specific AMC or MOC details the acceptable means of compliance for cooling‑loop segregation or power‑bus segregation for fly‑by‑wire computers. The applicant should propose a detailed means of compliance for EASA acceptance.

## Overall Compliance Summary

The design exhibits a sound foundation for propulsion independence by providing each motor with its own battery pack and controller and avoiding a common power bus between motor groups. However, two critical single‑point‑of‑failure issues render the aircraft non‑compliant with the SC-VTOL’s catastrophic‑failure‑criterion:  

- **Flight‑control computers** are all powered from a single 28 V DC bus, contrary to VTOL.2430(a)(1) and VTOL.2510(a)(1).  
- **The coolant loop** is shared across all eight motors, violating the independence requirement of VTOL.2430(a)(1) and the cooling‑adequacy requirement of EHPS.320.  

These issues must be resolved before the design can be considered compliant with the applicable certification basis (Category Enhanced). The independent motor‐battery architecture is commendable and meets the segregation goals.
